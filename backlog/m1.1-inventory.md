`michele-pasin` also appeared inside illustrative filename patterns —
`{folder-name}-michele-pasin-cover-letter-YYYY-MM-DD.md` and similar. Since M1.3 made
the slug configurable these were **factually wrong as well as personal**, which is why

across 9 files, now `<person-slug>`.

Three places keep the literal on purpose:

- `tools/build-example-fixture` and `tools/smoke-test` — the assertions that the
  author's name never reaches generated output. The literal *is* the check.
- `src/web/apps/tracker/tests.py` — filename fixtures for old-naming recognition. They
  represent real legacy files, and genericising them would lose exactly the property
  they exist to pin.

## What this pass changes about the estimate

§1 predicted the unchecked areas were `jobs/notes/` and "whether any `docs/*.md` embeds
personal specifics". `jobs/notes/` turned out to be a non-issue — it left with the data
root at step 2. `docs/` was right to flag: it carries both manual citations and
biographical references.

The two findings worth carrying forward are the ones the plan had already marked done:
the dump that was "cleared" but not deleted, and the manual references "removed from
every shipping file" that were only removed from some. **Both were recorded as
complete on the strength of the change that was made, not a check that it was
finished** — which is the same failure mode as `is_tailored_cv`'s test asserting the
implementation rather than the convention.

Suggested order: C1 first (licensing, mechanical), then C3 (mechanical, large), then
C2 (needs judgement), then C4.
