# Taking stock — Alex Rivera

Step 1 of a structured career-transition process: work out what you have, what you
want, and what you're worth, before looking at a single job ad.

**EXAMPLE DATA.** Alex Rivera is fictional. Replace this with your own by running
`/jobstudio stocktake`, which walks the sections one at a time.

## 1. Where I am now

Eight years in, six of them at Kestrel Financial, the last three as the person who owns
the data platform. The work is good and the company is fine. The reason for looking is
not escape — it's that the most satisfying year was the one spent teaching analytics
engineering, and that is not what the job is.

## 2. Achievements and USPs

- Took the critical pipeline path from six hours to 35 minutes, which changed what the
  business could do rather than just what the graphs looked like.
- Cut warehouse spend 40% with no throughput cost — the rare optimisation with a number
  attached that survived scrutiny.
- Built an enablement programme that moved 40+ analysts to owning their own models.
  Measured properly: models shipped without platform involvement went from none to most.
- Maintain a small open-source package with a real user base, and have the speaking
  history to go with it.

The USP is the combination rather than any one part: a platform engineer who can write
and teach, in a market where those are usually different people.

## 3. What I'm good at versus what I enjoy

Good at: pipeline architecture, cost work, incident practice, the unglamorous reliability
engineering that keeps a platform boring.

Enjoy: explaining it. Workshops, documentation, the talk, the moment an analyst ships
something without asking. The overlap is enablement, and that's the target.

## 4. Constraints

Bristol-based, hybrid or remote. Not relocating. Not commuting to London five days a
week. Salary floor is real but not the deciding factor between two good options.

## 5. Objectives

Move into a role where enablement is the job rather than a thing tolerated alongside it,
without abandoning the technical depth that makes it credible. Developer advocacy at a
company whose product I'd use anyway is the clearest expression of that.

## 6. Positioning by audience

- **Platform teams:** eight years of production platform ownership, with numbers.
- **DevRel teams:** a practitioner who already writes, speaks and maintains a package —
  not a communicator learning the domain.
- **Hiring managers worried about the switch:** the enablement programme was a real
  programme with real outcomes, not a side interest.

## 7. Narratives to have ready

- The six-hours-to-35-minutes migration, including what nearly went wrong.
- The on-call reduction, framed as deciding what *not* to alert on.
- The enablement programme, and why it needed to be someone's actual job.
- The honest "why leave" answer, which is the section 1 answer.
